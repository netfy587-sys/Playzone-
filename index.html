01

Know the board

The board is a cross with four coloured corners. Each corner is a home box where tokens wait. The cross is the shared track that every colour travels around.

Simple & Convenient to remember: your colour starts at its own coloured square, and finishes in the coloured column that leads to the centre.

Four home boxes, one shared track, one centre.

02

Roll a six to begin

Each turn, roll the die. A token can leave its home box only on a six. After that, every roll moves a token that many squares clockwise along the track.

In many houses, rolling a six also gives you an extra turn. It is a small reward that keeps the rounds exciting.

Faces 1 to 6. Only a six brings a token out.

03

Capture and be captured

If your token lands exactly on a square holding an opponent’s single token, that token is captured and sent back to its home box. Its owner then needs a new six to start again.

Squares marked with a star are usually safe, so tokens resting there cannot be captured.

04

Reach the centre

After a full lap, a token turns into its own coloured column and heads for the centre. The winner is the first player to bring all four tokens home.

Many families ask for an exact roll to enter the centre, so agree on this before you begin.

Smart play

Make Informed Choices with simple tips

Playzone keeps these ideas short, so every family can try them at the table.

Bring tokens out early

More tokens in play means more choices on every roll.

Use safe squares

Rest on a starred square when an opponent is close behind.

Think before capturing

A capture helps, but moving your own lead token home matters more.

Keep pairs together

In many houses, two tokens on one square block the track.

Watch the dice order

Check which opponent is next and where their tokens sit.

Stay friendly

It is a luck-and-choice pastime, best enjoyed with a smile.

History

Learn More Online: from ancient India to your table

A short timeline of a pastime that travelled across centuries.

Ancient India

Ludo grew out of Pachisi, a cross-shaped race that used cowrie shells in place of dice.

Royal courts

Elaborate versions were enjoyed in royal courts, and stories mention living pieces on large courtyards.

1896

A simplified version named Ludo was patented in England and spread to many homes.

Today

Families still gather around the board, and simple versions also exist on phones.

FAQ

Quick answers

Find useful information in one place.

How many people can play?

Usually two to four. Each person takes one colour and four tokens.

Why do I need a six to start?

It is the traditional rule: a token leaves its home box only after a six is rolled.

What happens when a token is captured?

It goes back to its home box, and its owner needs another six to bring it out again.

Are the rules the same everywhere?

Not exactly. Families and regions use small variations, so agree on the house rules before you begin.
